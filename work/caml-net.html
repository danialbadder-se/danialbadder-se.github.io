CAML-Net

What happens to a chest X-ray classifier when the accompanying radiology report is missing or mismatched? I explored that question with paired studies from Open-I.

How the model works

Each study supplies a chest image and a written radiology report. Frozen DenseNet-121 and BioBERT encoders extract visual and text features. Bidirectional cross-attention lets the two streams share information before predicting 14 report-derived observations.

  1. Encode both inputsMap image regions and report tokens into 512-dimensional representations.
  2. Compare and combineMeasure the discrepancy between shared features. Use conflict and uncertainty signals to adjust how much each input contributes.
  3. Evaluate each predictionProduce a probability and an uncertainty indicator for each observation. Estimate probability calibration on a separate split.

Experiment design

The final experiment used a Tesla T4 GPU, three training seeds (11, 22, 33), and nine configurations. The splits contained 2,676 training, 384 validation, 383 calibration, and 383 test studies. The run manifest records one checkpoint for each seed and configuration.

Fourteen observation targets include findings such as lung opacity and cardiomegaly, alongside “No Finding” and “Support Devices”. Of the 383 test studies, 376 had known weak targets for scoring. Unknown labels were masked.

What the comparison showed

Full CAML-Net reached a macro AUROC of 0.93759. Image only scored 0.75185, and text only reached 0.95318. The reports carried a strong signal for these report-derived labels. Full fusion also recorded a lower pooled calibration error than either single-input model.

What changed when reports went missing

I evaluated the full ensemble at five report-removal rates with fixed model weights. With 80% of reports missing, micro F1 fell from 0.82034 to 0.50368. Each point averages three masking draws.

Conflict, uncertainty, and limits

A controlled test swapped reports between 376 studies whose known labels differed. The model distinguished matched from swapped pairs with AUROC 0.67185. This tests sensitivity to an artificial mismatch. Clinical contradiction detection requires expert-reviewed cases.

The evaluation used 1,000 group-bootstrap draws and 2,000 paired permutations with Holm adjustment. The full model’s micro F1 interval was 0.78172–0.85667. Adjusted comparisons on Brier score, log loss, and micro F1 did not establish superiority over text only.

Open-I labels were mapped from the reports, some findings had only one or two positive test examples, and the test partition had been examined previously. Clinical validation and independent MIMIC-CXR evaluation remain open.
